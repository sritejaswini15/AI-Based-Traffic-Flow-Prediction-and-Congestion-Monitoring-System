Validation days (used to choose the model)

Models were fit on days {{ split.train[0] }}–{{ split.train[1] }} and compared on days {{ split.validation[0] }}–{{ split.validation[1] }}. The loss with the lowest mean validation MAE was kept: {{ metrics.selection.selected }}. The 80% ranges are the 10th and 90th percentiles of the validation errors, per junction and horizon. Errors are scaled by the square root of the forecast first, so ranges are wider at busy times, when counts vary more.

{{ results_table(metrics.validation, "Validation days") }}

Data handling

  • The source CSV ({{ ds.file }}) has one column per junction and no timestamps. Readings repeat on a 288-row cycle. At 5 minutes per reading that is exactly one day, and a weekly 2,016-row cycle is also present. Taking row 0 as midnight puts the daily low near 02:00 and the peak near 08:30. These clock times are inferred, not given by the source.
  • {{ "{:,}".format(ds.outage_steps) }} readings ({{ "%.1f"|format(ds.outage_steps / ds.steps * 100) }}%) are sensor outages, meaning {{ ds.outage_rule }}. They are treated as missing rather than as zero traffic, and no forecast is scored against them.
  • Features use only readings up to the moment of the forecast: recent counts and trends, the network total, the same time yesterday and last week, and the time of day being forecast. An automated test checks that no future reading leaks in.

Limits

  • Six junctions and 56 days only. There are no dates, so the model knows nothing about weekdays, holidays, weather or events.
  • There is no live feed. The dashboard replays recorded test days, and connecting real sensors would need a data-ingestion service.
  • The ranges come from 8 validation days. Rare events, such as an incident or a new outage pattern, will fall outside them more often than 20% of the time.
  • The level thresholds come from each junction's own history. A “heavy” reading at a quiet junction can still be a small number.
